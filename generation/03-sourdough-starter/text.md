## Sourdough starter in 7 days

A starter is just flour and water left to ferment until wild yeast and bacteria take hold. It takes about a week and a few minutes a day.

**You need:** a clean jar (about 500 ml), a kitchen scale, whole-wheat or rye flour to begin, plain white bread flour for feeding, and lukewarm water. Keep the jar somewhere warm, ideally 24–26 °C.

### Day-by-day plan

| Day | What to do | What you should see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Mix 50 g whole-wheat flour with 50 g water. Cover loosely. | A thick paste. Nothing else yet. |
| **2** | Leave it alone. Stir once. | A few small bubbles, maybe none. |
| **3** | Discard all but 50 g. Feed with 50 g white flour + 50 g water. | Bubbles, and a sharp or funky smell. |
| **4** | Discard to 50 g and feed 50 g + 50 g again. | Often goes quiet. This lull is normal. |
| **5** | Start feeding **twice a day**, 12 hours apart, same amounts. | Rises a little after each feed. |
| **6** | Feed twice again. | Rises predictably; smells pleasantly tangy. |
| **7** | Feed, then watch. | Doubles within 4–8 hours and is bubbly all the way through: it is ready. |

### How to tell it is ready

- It **doubles in volume** within 4–8 hours of a feed. Put a rubber band on the jar at the starting level to check.
- It smells tangy and slightly sweet, like yoghurt, and no longer of nail polish or old socks.
- A spoonful **floats** in a glass of water.

If it is not there on day 7, keep going with twice-daily feeds. In a cool kitchen it can take up to 14 days.

### If something looks wrong

- **Grey or brown liquid on top:** that is "hooch". The starter is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in, then feed.
- **No activity after day 4:** move it somewhere warmer and use a little whole-wheat flour in the feed.
- **Pink, orange or fuzzy patches:** that is mould or bad bacteria. Throw it out and start again.

### Keeping it alive

Once active, store it in the fridge and feed it once a week, or keep it on the counter and feed it daily. To bake, feed it and use it when it peaks.
