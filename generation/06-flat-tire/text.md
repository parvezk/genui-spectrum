## Changing a flat tire: roadside checklist

**Stay safe first.** If you are on a motorway or a narrow shoulder with fast traffic, do not change the wheel there. Get everyone out on the side away from traffic, stand behind the barrier and call roadside assistance.

### 1. Make the scene safe

- [ ] Pull over on **flat, firm ground**, well away from traffic.
- [ ] Switch on the **hazard lights** and put on a hi-vis vest if you have one.
- [ ] Apply the **parking brake**. Put the car in Park (or first gear for a manual).
- [ ] Get passengers out and well clear of the road.
- [ ] Place the **warning triangle** about 45 m (50 yards) behind the car.
- [ ] **Chock** the wheel diagonally opposite the flat with a wedge, brick or rock.

### 2. Change the wheel

- [ ] Get out the spare, jack and wheel wrench. Check the spare has air.
- [ ] Remove the hubcap if there is one. **Loosen each wheel nut half a turn** (anticlockwise) while the tire is still on the ground. Do not remove them yet.
- [ ] Put the jack under the **jacking point** nearest the flat (see the owner's manual) and raise the car until the tire is about 5 cm off the ground.
- [ ] **Never put any part of your body under the car** while it is on the jack.
- [ ] Remove the nuts, keep them together, and pull the wheel straight off.
- [ ] Fit the spare and **hand-tighten** the nuts in a **star pattern** (opposite nuts in turn, not round the circle).
- [ ] Lower the car until the tire touches the ground, then tighten the nuts fully with the wrench, again in a star pattern.
- [ ] Lower the car completely, remove the jack and give each nut a final check.

### 3. Before you drive off

- [ ] Stow the flat, jack, wrench, triangle and chock.
- [ ] Check the spare's pressure at the next fuel station.
- [ ] If it is a narrow **space-saver spare**, stay under **80 km/h (50 mph)** and replace it as soon as you can.
- [ ] Have the wheel nuts re-torqued after about 50 km.

### Call for help instead if

- You are on a motorway or cannot get clear of traffic.
- The ground is soft or sloping, or the jack will not sit securely.
- A wheel nut will not budge, or the car has no spare (many newer cars carry only a sealant kit).
