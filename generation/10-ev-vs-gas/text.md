## EV vs gas car: five-year cost of ownership

**Bottom line:** at 12,000 miles a year the two are almost identical after five years. The gas car is ahead by just **$100**. The EV breaks even about one month into year six and is cheaper every year after that.

| | EV | Gas car |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Purchase price | $38,000 | $30,000 |
| Energy or fuel per year (12,000 mi) | $480 | $1,560 |
| Maintenance per year | $400 | $900 |
| **Running cost per year** | **$880** | **$2,460** |
| Running cost over 5 years | $4,400 | $12,300 |
| **Total after 5 years** | **$42,400** | **$42,300** |

### How it adds up

- **EV:** 12,000 mi × $0.04 = $480 energy, plus $400 maintenance = $880 a year. Over five years: $38,000 + 5 × $880 = **$42,400**.
- **Gas:** 12,000 mi × $0.13 = $1,560 fuel, plus $900 maintenance = $2,460 a year. Over five years: $30,000 + 5 × $2,460 = **$42,300**.

The EV costs $8,000 more up front and saves $1,580 a year to run, so it needs 8,000 ÷ 1,580 ≈ **5.1 years** (about 60,800 miles) to catch up.

### What would tip it

- **Drive more and the EV wins sooner.** At 15,000 miles a year the EV saves $1,850 a year and is ahead by $1,250 at five years.
- **Keep the car longer.** Every extra year adds $1,580 to the EV's lead.
- **Not included here:** resale value, incentives or tax credits, insurance, home charger installation and financing. Any of these can be larger than the $100 gap, so check them for your situation.
